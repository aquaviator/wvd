# WVD web content standard 1.0.0

Authority: founder instructions on 4 October 2026. Applies to every web-facing
project with creative content. Every page must have a relevant call to action.
This is a shared development requirement, not permission to publish, send social
posts, enable paid media generation or expose private customer content.

## One topic, one consistent content bundle

Each new article or blog post has a descriptive feature visual, a short video
highlighting its key context, and a complete written article. Reuse the same
checked source material across all three. Prepare a vertical short-form reel
alongside the site video where social distribution is relevant; preparing it
does not authorise publication. HyperFrames is the chosen video authoring
direction. Local rendering is assessed before hosted/paid features; no new
provider subscription or billable media API is enabled implicitly.

Recommended page order: title and brief answer, descriptive feature visual,
watch option, readable article, sources/review information and contextual CTA.
The reader can jump to watching or reading. Text remains fully useful without
watching. Videos use controls, descriptive posters, captions and a transcript;
no unsolicited autoplay or dependence on sound. Keep the initial page light.

Infographics are optional: use them to clarify a process, comparison, checklist
or verified data. A decorative graphic is not a substitute for evidence. Provide
the information in accessible text too. Sources and dates accompany numerical
graphics, and the visual must not imply measurements or results we do not have.

## CTA on every page

Choose one clear primary action appropriate to the page and the reader's stage.
An article may invite a relevant enquiry or next guide; services may invite a
discussion; booking offers selection/confirmation/status; sign-in offers sign-in;
a policy page offers the appropriate contact or return action. Secondary actions
are subordinate. This rule includes functional pages; it does not force a sales
pitch into private workflows or legal information. Preserve unavailable purchase
states and never imply an unavailable service is operating. CTAs must work with
keyboard input and have clear outcomes; avoid deceptive urgency and dead links.

## Authority, accuracy and discovery

Give the article an identifiable author/publisher, clear key points, original
useful context and supporting sources for factual claims. Record publication,
material update and actual fact-check dates separately. Never refresh timestamps
automatically to make stale material look current. Link the article, media,
script/transcript and source record to the same content version; a factual change
triggers review of every affected format. Flag superseded material and correct
or withdraw obsolete media. AI assistance is disclosed where useful; it does
not constitute independent review or establish authority by itself.

Use crawlable public HTML, stable canonical/media URLs, meaningful image text,
captions/transcripts and truthful Article/ImageObject/VideoObject metadata where
appropriate. Only describe real assets and dates. For video-search eligibility,
assess a dedicated watch page, stable poster/content URL and sitemap requirements;
an embedded clip in a text-led article does not guarantee a video-search result.
Do not promise indexing, AI citations or rankings for any element. Preserve the
founder's public-search/AI-search and training-crawler preferences; public metadata
does not override authentication or make private portals eligible for discovery.

## Definition of done and current implementation state

A new creative post is ready only when its visual, final rendered video, captions,
transcript, complete text, CTA and metadata agree, assets/licences are verified,
sources and review dates are recorded, and mobile/accessibility/links/media checks
pass against the exact version. Inspect the exported video itself, not just the
editor preview. Check public indexing separately after an authorised deployment;
never claim it from a local build. Existing articles need a tracked media retrofit;
recording this standard does not fabricate missing video assets.

Reuse assessed: existing Astro layouts/article content, product media manifests,
caption tooling and video components. The current product facade creates its
video only after a click. That loading approach needs assessment against video
discovery requirements before it becomes the shared creative-content player.
HyperFrames dependency, licence, rendering and final-media evidence remain work
to integrate; there is no operational HyperFrames pipeline claim yet.
